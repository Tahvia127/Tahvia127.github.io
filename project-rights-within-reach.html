Rights Within Reach:
Legal information, in five languages

A working demo of a free, multilingual web app that helps Illinois residents understand their rights around eviction, debt, home repairs, and public benefits. Retrieval-augmented over 130+ verified legal-aid sources, read aloud in the user's language, and always pointing to a real organization that can help.

Overview

Legal information — never legal advice.

The people who most need to understand their legal rights are the least able to pay for the answer. Legal aid exists, but it is scattered across dozens of organizations, written at a reading level most people struggle with, and almost never available in the language they speak at home.

Rights Within Reach answers questions about housing and rent, money and debt, home repairs, and public benefits like SNAP and Medicaid. Every answer is grounded in verified sources, cites them, and ends by naming a real organization that can help. It is not a law firm, and the interface says so at both ends of every response.

Status: this is a working demo, not a launched service. It covers Illinois today, and the current gates before a public launch are native-speaker review of the machine-drafted translations and expansion to additional states.

How an answer gets built

Guided triage → area → ZIP → subject → question
Retrieval → ~940 chunks from 130+ tracked Illinois sources (Chroma)
Web check → when corpus coverage is thin, allow-listed gov / legal-aid domains only
Structured answer → plain-language response + next steps + who to contact
Confidence rating → high / medium / low, surfaced to the user

The design decisions that mattered

  • Refusal is a feature. Immigration, criminal, and family law questions — and anything signalling danger — are declined and routed to the right referral organization rather than guessed at. Getting this wrong hurts people.
  • Five languages, honestly labelled. English, Spanish, Chinese, Tagalog, and Vietnamese, with a visible "machine-assisted, under review" banner until native speakers verify each one. Claiming fluency you haven't verified is its own kind of harm.
  • Read aloud, per section. Browser text-to-speech in the selected language, with speed and voice controls — literacy is a barrier independent of language.
  • Private by default. No login, and no raw questions or IP addresses logged.
  • Fresh. A daily 8 AM CT job re-fetches every source and flags changed legal text for review, so answers don't quietly go stale.

Testing it

A 21-case safety and quality benchmark runs against the pipeline, checking that in-scope questions get grounded answers with citations and that out-of-scope questions get refused and referred rather than answered.
