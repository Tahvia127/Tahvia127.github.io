PolicyGuesser:
Would You Have Voted Yes?

An interactive web game built on real United Nations Security Council and General Assembly resolutions. Players read a plain-English summary, see who argued for and against, cast a vote, and watch the actual country-by-country outcome animate on a D3 world map.

Role

Solo build

Showcase

UChicago Tech Showcase

Overview

Translating diplomacy into a game loop.

Most people have no idea how the UN actually works. The information is public, but the format is dense and designed for diplomats. PolicyGuesser closes that gap by turning real resolutions into a fast, surprising, shareable experience. The learning happens through plot twists: discovering the U.S. voted against a resolution condemning nuclear weapons, or that an obviously-good resolution failed because of a single veto.

Core game loop

  • Resolution card with a plain-English summary, no jargon.
  • Country positions visualized on an interactive D3 world map.
  • Player votes Yes, No, or Abstain.
  • Outcome reveal animates the real country-by-country breakdown.
  • "What happened next" card explains the real-world consequence.

Engagement mechanics

Streaks & Tiers

Difficulty modes from beginner outcomes to expert controversial splits.

Daily Challenge

One featured resolution a day, with a global leaderboard.

Country Profiles

Click any nation to see its full UN voting history.

Shareable Cards

"I voted with the U.S. 80% of the time", built for social.

Data sources

UN Bibliographic Information System (UNBIS), the UN Digital Library API, the Voeten dataset on UN voting (Harvard Dataverse), and ACLED for post-resolution conflict events. All public.

Tech stack
